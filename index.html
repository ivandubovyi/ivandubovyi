17 years ago

How my parents met

My dad first saw my mom in a local newspaper. She had earned the President’s Award for her success in college. He didn’t think much of it back then, but little did he know it was the beginning of their big love story.

The second time was at a summer camp, where Dad saw Mom in real life for the first time. She was rushing from one class to the next, and she still managed to catch his eye. He didn’t think much of that either. But now it was twice.

The summer passed and the school year began again. Dad was running for leadership positions at the college, and he needed smart, ambitious people on his team. The first person who came to mind was Mom, one of the smartest students on campus. He found her walking calmly down the street, came up to her, and asked her to join his team.

And who would’ve thought: she politely declined and walked away, with no idea that the person asking was her future husband.

They had many more encounters before they started dating. They had ups and they had downs, but ever since they’ve known each other, destiny has led them to where they are now.

17 years of union, 17 years of love, and many more to come. Happy anniversary, Mom and Dad.

What I build

My Coding Journey

Programming started out as a hobby for me, but now - it's a lifestyle. I started on Scratch, learning it step by step, in an online school called ItgenIo. At the age of 8, I worked as hard as I could, gaining 100 hours of lessons by the age of 10, and being able to do complex projects using Scratch.

As I started diving deeper into the complex world of technology, I began learning about HTML & CSS. Using these languages, I learned about building websites. I could build simple algorithms that would help in building of a complex website. Using HTML & CSS, I could build a big part of this website.

After 96 hours of joy and pleasure using HTML and CSS, I transitioned on working with JavaScript. For the first couple lessons - it was hard. Learning a new language is always challenging, but JavaScript was especially hard for me. I was confused at first, but my coaches helped me figure everything out, and I spent 118 hours learning JavaScript.

Recently, I've decided to move to a new language. Python, is something that I am really interested in, as I can make games and learn more about AI using Python. Everyday I'm exploring Python, learning about new cool stuff and trying out new projects.

Ed-tech · Founder

Algebridge

Algebridge is a student-led initiative I founded to help people who are struggling with Algebra 1. The site lays out our mission and opens into an interactive learning platform with videos, step-by-step help, and a fun-house mini-game where you earn points called Bridgeys as you practice. The whole idea is to make sure nobody gets left behind at the exact moment math starts to get hard.

Social · Web

Somba

Somba is a network for ambitious teenagers, built around collaborators instead of followers. Think LinkedIn, but for teen builders: you apply to join, and members gather in small groups called Nets rather than one endless feed. The whole idea is people over followers, work over performance, and small close networks.

Visit Somba
Messaging · Web

Sentloop

Sentloop makes sure you never miss a birthday or a holiday. You add the people who matter, and it remembers every date, writes something that sounds like you, and gets it there on the day. Where a platform lets apps send for you, the message goes out on its own. Everywhere else it hands you the message ready to go, and tells you which is which. The whole idea is staying close to people without holding all of it in your head.

On the field

Refereeing

My refereeing journey started in 2025, because of my passion to soccer. I always wanted to try a different role in soccer, and refereeing was the perfect opportunity for me. I started learning about it.

Over the summer of 2025, I completed a course of lessons, and in mid-fall, I officiated my first game. It was fun and interesting experience, being the center of the game I always loved and making decisions on the field that would decide the outcome of the game

As of November 2025, I refereed over 20 games, from ages u8 to u18. I worked as an assistant referee, and as the center referee, and I don't plan to stop. Now, being a referee is a lifestyle for me. I can be a part of the game, while also making key decisions. That's what I love the most about refereeing.

Education has always been important to me. Here's my academic journey so far:
